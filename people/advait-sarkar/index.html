About

Advait Sarkar directs WITS Lab. He is an affiliated lecturer at the University of Cambridge and an honorary lecturer at University College London, and was previously a researcher at Microsoft, where his work contributed to features in Excel, PowerPoint, and Teams.

His research studies how to design human interfaces for artificial intelligence, and how to help people program and interact with data. His recent work examines how generative AI affects cognition, knowledge work, organisations, and society, and how AI might challenge and provoke us so that we think more critically, rather than less.

He spoke at TEDAI Vienna about AI as a tool for thought, advises governments and regulators on technology, automation, and data, and has designed and taught graduate courses at Cambridge on human-centred AI and on critical approaches to technology.

Highlighted work

  • AI Should Challenge, Not Obey

    Today’s AI assistants are built to do what they are told. This essay argues that they should act as provocateurs instead, questioning our work and offering alternatives so that we think more critically, not less.

    Communications of the ACM, 2024 Cover story
  • The Impact of Generative AI on Critical Thinking

    A survey of 319 knowledge workers, who shared 936 real examples of using generative AI at work. Higher confidence in AI was associated with less critical thinking, and higher self-confidence with more.

    CHI 2025
  • Vibe coding: programming through conversation with artificial intelligence

    The first empirical study of vibe coding, in which programmers build software mainly by conversing with AI. Vibe coding does not remove the need for expertise; it redistributes it.

    PPIG 2025
  • It’s Time to Think About Time

    Reading AI as a “chronotechnology”, this paper draws on the philosophy of speed to ask how acceleration reshapes knowledge workers’ relationships with time, power, and risk.

    BCS HCI 2026
