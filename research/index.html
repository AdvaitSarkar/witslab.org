AI as a tool for thought

Many AI tools focus on generating content or automating processes. Though useful, these systems can affect how we think, learn, build skills, and develop expertise. We ask how AI might instead help people think better.

The work we build on

The lab’s agenda grows out of several years of prior research on artificial intelligence, programming, and knowledge work.

Critical thinking with AI

  • AI Should Challenge, Not Obey

    Today’s AI assistants are built to do what they are told. This essay argues that they should act as provocateurs instead, questioning our work and offering alternatives so that we think more critically, not less.

    Communications of the ACM, 2024 Cover story
  • The Impact of Generative AI on Critical Thinking: Self-Reported Reductions in Cognitive Effort and Confidence Effects From a Survey of Knowledge Workers

    A survey of 319 knowledge workers, who shared 936 real examples of using generative AI at work. Higher confidence in AI was associated with less critical thinking, and higher self-confidence with more.

    CHI 2025
  • The Metacognitive Demands and Opportunities of Generative AI

    Deciding what to ask, judging what comes back, and knowing when to rely on it: generative AI places heavy metacognitive demands on its users. This paper sets out how AI systems could be designed to support and reduce those demands.

    CHI 2024 Best paper
  • “It makes you think”: Provocations Help Restore Critical Thinking to AI-Assisted Knowledge Work

    Provocations are brief prompts that critique AI suggestions and propose alternatives. In a controlled study, they induced critical and metacognitive thinking, pointing to practical designs for keeping people engaged.

    arXiv preprint, 2025

Knowledge work

  • It’s Time to Think About Time: Artificial Intelligence, the Speed of Organisational Culture, and the Social Acceleration of Knowledge Work

    AI is changing not only the pace of work, but the pace of change itself. Reading AI as a “chronotechnology”, this paper draws on the philosophy of speed to ask how acceleration reshapes knowledge workers’ relationships with time, power, and risk.

    BCS HCI 2026
  • Exploring Perspectives on the Impact of Artificial Intelligence on the Creativity of Knowledge Work: Beyond Mechanised Plagiarism and Stochastic Parrots

    Critics call generative AI mechanised plagiarism, but creativity and originality have always resisted definition. Drawing on literary criticism, art history, and copyright law, this paper argues that AI shifts knowledge work from material production to critical integration.

    CHIWORK 2023
  • AI Could Have Written This: Birth of a Classist Slur in Knowledge Work

    Why do people disparage work by saying that AI could have written it? This paper argues that AI shaming stems from class anxiety among knowledge workers, and polices who gets to join their ranks.

    CHI 2025 Extended Abstracts
  • “If You’re Very Clever, No One Knows You’ve Used It”: The Social Dynamics of Developing Generative AI Literacy in the Workplace

    Interviews with 19 knowledge workers about how they learn to use generative AI at work. Hiding signs of AI use was seen as a mark of expertise, which in turn reduced knowledge sharing and transparency.

    CHIWORK 2026 Honourable mention

Programming and data

  • Vibe coding: programming through conversation with artificial intelligence

    The first empirical study of vibe coding, in which programmers build software mainly by conversing with AI. Vibe coding does not remove the need for expertise; it redistributes it towards managing context, evaluating code rapidly, and knowing when to take back manual control.

    PPIG 2025
  • What is it like to program with artificial intelligence?

    Programming with large language models resembles compilation, pair programming, and searching for code to reuse, but it is none of these. This paper argues that it is a genuinely new way of programming, with its own distinct properties and challenges.

    PPIG 2022
  • Will Code Remain a Relevant User Interface for End-User Programming with Generative AI Models?

    If anyone can generate code from a natural-language prompt, do programming languages still matter for non-experts? This essay proposes the “generative shift hypothesis” and sets out why code may remain a valuable interface.

    Onward! 2023
  • “What It Wants Me To Say”: Bridging the Abstraction Gap Between End-User Programmers and Code-Generating Large Language Models

    Only some ways of phrasing a request work well with code-generating AI, and non-experts struggle to learn which. Translating generated code back into systematic, predictable language helped spreadsheet users understand what the model could do and how to ask for it.

    CHI 2023 Honourable mention

Human interfaces for AI

  • Intention Is All You Need

    Generative AI promises that anyone can turn intentions directly into software. This paper questions that promise: AI tends to homogenise intentions, and forming an intention is hard in the first place, which points towards designs for more intentional tools.

    PPIG 2024
  • Enough With “Human-AI Collaboration”

    Calling our interactions with AI “collaboration” is well-intentioned but misleading, because it obscures the human labour behind AI, much of it in the Global South. Viewing AI as a tool is more accurate, and fairer.

    CHI 2023 Extended Abstracts
  • Large Language Models Cannot Explain Themselves

    When asked to explain their output, language models produce text that does not reflect how that output was actually generated. This paper calls these “exoplanations”, and argues that they are better used to provoke critical thinking than to understand the model.

    CHI 2024 Workshop on Human-Centered Explainable AI
  • Should Computers Be Easy To Use? Questioning the Doctrine of Simplicity in User Interface Design

    That software should be easy to learn and use is rarely questioned. This paper traces where the doctrine of simplicity came from, shows how it limits software and privileges some cultures over others, and proposes “negotiated complexity” instead.

    CHI 2023 Extended Abstracts
